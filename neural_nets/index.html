Neural Network Visualizer

Draw a digit and watch a real, trained neural network classify it — neuron by neuron, in 3D.

What's going on here?

This demo runs a small multi-layer perceptron (MLP) — one of the simplest kinds of neural network — trained to recognize handwritten digits from the classic MNIST dataset. Draw a digit on the 28×28 grid and your drawing becomes the network's input layer: 784 numbers, one per pixel, each between 0 and 1.

From there, each layer computes a weighted sum of the previous layer's outputs, adds a learned bias, and passes the result through an activation function (ReLU) that decides how strongly that neuron "fires." The 3D scene shows this happening live: spheres are neurons, colored by activation strength; cylinders are the strongest connections between them. By the time the signal reaches the 10-neuron output layer, it's been turned into a probability for each digit 0–9.

  • It's just arithmetic: every prediction is a chain of multiplications, additions, and simple thresholding — matrix multiplication with extra steps. There's no reasoning happening inside; the network has only ever learned statistical patterns in pixels.
  • Weights ARE the model: the ~109,000 numbers that make up this network's connections are the entire "knowledge" it has. Training is nothing more than nudging those numbers until predictions get better.
  • Why it matters for security/ML: because a model only reacts to pixel patterns, it can be fooled by inputs a human would never mistake for the target class (adversarial examples) — and it silently inherits every bias present in its training data.

Network architecture

The default export is a small, fully-connected network — deliberately compact so the 3D view stays smooth:

→
128 Hidden 1 ReLU
→

→
10 Output Softmax

How to use it

  • Draw: click-and-drag on the grid (top left) to sketch a digit, or click a digit button below the grid to load a random real MNIST sample instead.
  • Watch: activations light up neuron-by-neuron as your drawing propagates through the network in real time.
  • Read the prediction: the bar chart (top right) shows the network's confidence for every digit 0–9.
  • Click a neuron: inspect exactly which inputs and weights fed into it, and where its output goes next.
  • Scrub the timeline: drag the slider at the bottom to rewind training itself — from random, untrained weights (10% accuracy, i.e. a coin flip across 10 digits) through 35 checkpoints up to 50× the dataset, watching accuracy climb.
  • Orbit the camera: left-drag to rotate, right-drag to pan, scroll to zoom.
